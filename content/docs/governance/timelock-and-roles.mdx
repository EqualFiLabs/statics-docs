The Phase 1 deployment configured the protocol contract under the deployment
signer, created four public pools, and transferred ownership to the governance
Safe. At the [verification block](/docs/reference/phase-one-deployment), the Safe
still owned the protocol. `StaticsTimelock` was deployed, but the final ownership
handoff had not occurred. After that handoff, protocol upgrades, economic
configuration, lifecycle release, hook-fee changes, and treasury or guardian
changes flow through the timelock. Fee-paid public pool creation needs no
governance transaction. The separate Dollar Core contract arrives in Phase 3.

## Delay

The Robinhood mainnet timelock had a **24-hour** minimum delay at verification. Local development uses a two-minute delay. Verify the current deployed delay and protocol owner before treating a governance operation as executable.

:::warning
A local development delay is not a production governance setting. Check the deployed contract and scheduled operation before acting.
:::

## Roles

| Role | Held by | Power |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proposer | Configured multisig | Schedule timelocked operations |
| Canceller | Governance Safe at Phase 1 verification | Cancel scheduled operations |
| Executor | Open (`address(0)`) | Execute once delay elapses |
| Guardian | Governance Safe at Phase 1 verification | Pause exposure-increasing actions; basket quarantine arrives in Phase 2 |
| Treasury | Configured | Receives terminal fee distributions |

At this deployment, the governance Safe also holds the guardian and timelock canceller roles. These are separate permissions even though one address holds them. The timelock is its own admin.

## What the timelock controls

After the Safe-to-timelock handoff, the timelock controls protocol upgrades, economic configuration, lifecycle
release and decommissioning, hook fee configuration, and treasury or guardian
changes. Reward-asset selection is a PositionNFT owner action and requires no
governance admission or retirement.

## Protocol upgrades

Changing facet routing is the sole implementation upgrade mechanism. Facets share the
common OpenZeppelin persistent reentrancy slot under delegatecall; flash loans
add a separate transient guard domain and acquire the persistent slot only for
their transfer/accounting phases. Selector routing and ERC-165 declarations must
be updated together.

Core bootstrap finalization validates wiring, pins the periphery as the initial
managed recovery holder, and clears bootstrap authority. The Core owner may
explicitly add or revoke other managed recovery holders; revocation returns the
holder's expired positions to ordinary permissionless recovery. Finalization
does **not** make either protocol contract immutable.

## Toward final immutability

The intended release lifecycle is upgradeable development followed by an
independent contract and governance audit, reduction of retained powers, transfer
to final governance, and a final reviewed upgrade that removes upgrade authority
from both protocol contracts. After that, "immutable" means facet routing and
implementation code are fixed; any deliberately retained parameter or emergency
powers must be enumerated separately.
