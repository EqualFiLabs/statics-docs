Current `statics` master recognizes two protocol-pool classes:

- **basket canonical pools**, created atomically with a basket and associated
  with one constituent; and
- **general pools**, created independently between any two compatible ERC-20s
  with no basket association.

Both use the installed Statics hook, zero native Uniswap v4 LP fee, bilateral
Statics fees, full-range protocol-owned liquidity, and the same optional LP
reward custody. A normalized PoolId registry lets the hook, liquidity manager,
and reward system validate either class through one bounded lookup.

:::warning Not deployed at the recorded testnet addresses
The Robinhood testnet deployment currently exposes the earlier
governance-created protocol-pool model. Permissionless general pools, creator
authorization, and the APIs on this page are current source behavior awaiting a
matching deployment.
:::

## Creation switch

`poolCreationFee()` is independent from basket and PositionNFT creation fees.
It is also the permissionless-creation switch:

| Value | Who may call `createPool` |
| --- | --- |
| `0` | Diamond owner only, with zero `msg.value` |
| Positive | Any caller paying the exact amount, forwarded to treasury |

Zero therefore means disabled public creation, not free public creation.

Call `quotePool(params)` before `createPool(params, creatorAuthorization)`. The
quote returns the sorted PoolKey, PoolId, normalized initial price, bilateral
fee rate, exact creation fee, and EIP-712 authorization digest. General-pool
creation selects:

- two distinct non-native compatible ERC-20s;
- tick spacing from 1 through 32,767;
- an initial raw-unit B-per-A square-root price;
- PoolId-local input and output hook fees totaling at most 200 BPS;
- an immutable creator, unordered nonce, and deadline.

The PoolKey always has native v4 fee zero and the installed Statics hook.
Different tick spacings create different PoolIds; price or hook-fee changes
alone do not.

## Creator authorization

The named creator can create directly without a signature. A relayer needs an
EIP-712 authorization validated for EOAs or ERC-1271 contract wallets. The
signature binds the PoolId, normalized price, both fee rates, creator, nonce,
deadline, chain ID, and Diamond address. It does not bind the fee-paying
relayer, so copying a transaction cannot substitute a different creator or
market configuration.

Cancel unused signed intents with `invalidatePoolCreationNonce` and inspect
them with `isPoolCreationNonceUsed`.

## Creation is not liquidity provision

`createPool` registers and initializes the market but does not require an
initial liquidity seed. A general pool may begin with zero liquidity. Users can
then create ordinary PositionManager NFTs through the installed manager; fee
activity can form the pool's first hook-owned permanent-liquidity position.

Registration does **not** make either token basket backing, Dollar collateral,
or a borrowable asset. Pool price, TWAP, and liquidity are not Statics solvency
inputs. General pools receive no basket-staker allocation and cannot use the
basket borrow-to-liquidity paths.

## Fees and creator revenue

Each PoolId has its own input/output hook-fee rate. General pools share the
global general-pool allocation profile. The creator always receives the fixed
500-BPS share of collected bilateral fees in each pool currency, claimed
through `claimCreatorRevenue`. Governance cannot redirect that accrued credit,
and it remains claimable after decommissioning.

See [Swap-fee hook](/docs/liquidity/swap-fee-hook) for the complete profiles and
fallback rules.

## Discovery and decommissioning

Index `ProtocolPoolCreated` rather than expecting an unbounded onchain pool
array. Use `protocolPool`, `isProtocolPool`, `protocolPoolCreator`, and
`protocolPoolFeeRate` for bounded reads.

Only timelocked governance can call `decommissionGeneralPool`. The irreversible
transition stops later swaps and managed LP actions, releases permanent
liquidity to treasury accounting, and preserves user LP principal, creator
credits, and earned rewards. The pool creator has no administrative authority.
